## Artefact 1: Six killed in Nepal amid Gen Z protests after social media ban: All to know

https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2025/9/8/six-killed-in-nepal-amid-gen-z-protests-after-social-media-ban-all-to-know

![Timeline diagram of Nepal's 2025 social media ban and the Gen Z protests it triggered](images/ep4-artefact1-diagram.svg)

### Summary of the artefact

Shamim (2025) reports that Nepal blocked 26 platforms over a missed registration deadline, officially to curb fake accounts and cybercrime. Protests over the ban and corruption turned deadly when police fired on crowds, killing at least 19 before it was lifted.

### Justification on why I chose the artefact

A trend exposing politicians' children living lavishly was already spreading before any of this (Dudraj 2025), and the ban arrived right after. That timing is hard to take at face value. It shows what centralised control looks like when the people doing the filtering have something to lose themselves, which is why I land on a consortium blockchain in Artefact 4.

## Artefact 2: Powers of Online Safety Act tested in Federal Court case

https://www.hrlc.org.au/case-summaries/2024-06-19-esafety-commissioner-v-x-corp/

![Diagram comparing the geo-block Australia got against the global takedown eSafety sought](images/ep4-artefact2-diagram.svg)

### Summary of the artefact

Human Rights Law Centre (2024) reports on *eSafety Commissioner v X Corp*. eSafety wanted 65 links to graphic stabbing footage removed worldwide, but X had already blocked it for Australian users, and the Federal Court ruled that sufficient under the Online Safety Act.

### Justification on why I chose the artefact

Mill allowed government intervention only to prevent harm to others, and violent footage looks like the easy case for that. I assumed it was uncontroversial. The ruling did not argue about harm at all, it argued about reach, which showed me a limit Mill never had to think about: whose harm, in whose country.

## Artefact 3: Government censorship comes to Bluesky, but not its third-party apps yet

https://techcrunch.com/2025/04/23/government-censorship-comes-to-bluesky-but-not-its-third-party-apps-yet

![Diagram showing a government demand reaching one app on a shared protocol while other apps on the same protocol still show the content](images/ep4-artefact3-diagram.svg)

### Summary of the artefact

Perez (2025) reports that Bluesky agreed to a Turkish government request, hiding around 60 accounts from users in Turkey on national security grounds. Bluesky's posts live on an open protocol, not inside its app, and the filter was applied by that app alone. Other apps reading the same protocol never applied it, so the accounts stayed visible.

### Justification on why I chose the artefact

I picked this because it tests the idea I land on in Artefact 4 rather than just agreeing with it. Bluesky still gave in, so spreading a system out does not stop a government applying pressure. What it stops is that one yes being the end of the matter, because no single company was holding the only copy.

## Artefact 4: Workshop Personal Reflection

![Diagram comparing a single central authority against a consortium that must agree before a takedown](images/ep4-artefact4-diagram.svg)

### Summary of the artefact

One of the things the tutor put into the spotlight was the Daily Stormer case. GoDaddy and then Google cancelled the site's domain, but Cloudflare kept it for days. What changed Cloudflare's mind was not the content: it was Daily Stormer publicly claiming Cloudflare secretly supported their ideology, which put the company in a bad spot.

### Justification on why I chose the artefact

What caught my eye is that the decision came down to reputation, not belief. Cloudflare only moved once the association was advertised to the world. That made me ask who should be making these calls at all. A single central authority sounds cleaner, but if that authority is corrupt the bias just moves up one level. My own idea is a consortium blockchain, where a group of independent members runs the ledger together instead of one owner, with AI models built on different ideologies sitting as those members, so a takedown only goes ahead if they agree. Artefact 3 shows that would not stop the pressure, but it would stop one company's decision being the only one that counts.
